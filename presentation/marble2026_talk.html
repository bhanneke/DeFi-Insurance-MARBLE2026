This paper

The mechanism must solve three design problems

Problem 1 · Pricing
Hack risk has no actuarial table
Loss histories are sparse and heterogeneous; threats evolve faster than models recalibrate. Today, premiums come from assessors on thin data, at several percent per year.
Goal: prices that equal risk-neutral hack probabilities
Problem 2 · Capital
Risk capital leaves when risk rises
Fixed returns give capital no reason to stay in stress, so underwriting capacity is scarcest exactly when it is needed most.
Goal: a risk premium that adjusts dynamically to utilization and priced risk
Problem 3 · Incentives
Coverage creates moral hazard
An insured party invests less in its own security. Today's user-bought cover leaves the protocol, which controls security, without a stake in the policy.
Goal: coverage that preserves the incentive to invest in security

Problem 1 · Pricing

HACK token prices reveal market-implied hack probabilities

Goal
Prices equal risk-neutral hack probabilities
Enforced by
Competitive speculation; no-arbitrage in the binary set \(\mathrm{HACK}+\mathrm{NOHACK}=\$1\)
  • Dispersed security knowledge is revealed through trading, and traders are paid for it
  • The term structure yields \(\hat\lambda\); aggregation gives the risk index \(P_{\mathrm{risk}}\)
  • Dedicated, operator-governed markets with permissioned access

Prices rise with maturity; fitting the term structure yields \(\hat\lambda\) and, aggregated, \(P_{\mathrm{risk}}\).

Problem 2 · Capital

The yield share adjusts LP returns to utilization and priced risk

Goal
A dynamic risk premium: LP capital earns more when it carries more
Enforced by
The yield-share function \(\gamma\), a protocol rule measurable on-chain
\(\gamma\,(U,\;P_{\mathrm{risk}})\in[0,1]\) — increasing in both arguments
  • \(C_{\mathrm{LP}}\) — the risk capital liquidity providers supply to the insurance pool
  • \(U = \mathrm{coverage} \,/\, C_{\mathrm{LP}}\) — utilization: aggregate outstanding coverage relative to that capital; high \(U\) means capital is scarce
  • \(P_{\mathrm{risk}}\) — the market-implied risk index aggregated from HACK prices
  • LP yield therefore rises through two channels: capital scarcity and market-priced risk

Problem 2 · Capital

The resulting capital adjustment is self-stabilizing

  • Self-stabilizing: returns above \(r_{\mathrm{market}}+\rho_{\mathrm{LP}}\) attract capital, which lowers \(U\) and compresses \(\gamma\)
  • Role separation: capital providers are barred from HACK positions and cannot hedge the insured exposure

Problem 3 · Incentives

The insured protocol posts the first-loss capital

Goal
Moral hazard prevention: covered protocols keep investing in security
Enforced by
Forfeitable first-loss collateral and security-scaled coverage
  • Coverage is concave in collateral: \(\mathrm{coverage} = \mu\, C_{\mathrm{C}}^{\theta}(1+\xi)\), \(\theta\in(0,1)\)
  • Verifiable security investment raises \(\xi\) and buys more coverage per dollar of collateral
  • In the no-hack state, protocols receive a share of pool yield, lowering their net cost of coverage

Losses hit the protocol's own collateral before any pool capital.

Theorem 1 · A Nash equilibrium exists

Under quasi-concave payoffs, a Nash equilibrium \((C_{\mathrm{C}}^{*},\,C_{\mathrm{LP}}^{*},\,S^{*})\) exists for any continuous \(\gamma(U,P_{\mathrm{risk}})\in[0,1]\) increasing in both arguments.
\(\pi_{\mathrm{LP}} = \gamma\,(1-\varphi)\,Y_{\mathrm{total}} - p_{\mathrm{hack}}\,\mathbb{E}\bigl[(\min(\mathrm{coverage},\mathrm{Loss})-C_{\mathrm{C}})^{+}\bigr] - C_{\mathrm{LP}}\,\mathbb{E}[r_{\mathrm{market}}]\) (Eq. 11 — the protocol objective, Eq. 9, is analogous)
  1. Prop. 1 pins HACK prices to risk-neutral probabilities; the pricing layer enters the game only through the continuous index \(P_{\mathrm{risk}}\)
  2. Wealth constraints make strategy sets compact and convex; payoffs are continuous (coverage function, bounded \(\gamma\))
  3. Quasi-concavity in each player's own strategy: standard sufficient conditions for existence in continuous games apply

Proposition 1 · Prices equal risk-neutral hack probabilities

Under competitive markets with risk-neutral speculators, equilibrium HACK prices reveal the risk-neutral probabilities of a material exploit.
\(V_{\mathrm{HACK}}(T) = p_{\mathrm{hack}}(T)\cdot \mathrm{DF}(T), \qquad p_{\mathrm{hack}}(T) = 1 - e^{-\lambda T}\) (Eq. 12 — fair value of the binary claim)
  1. Competitive trading among admitted speculators: beliefs and the implied probability converge
  2. Complete binary set per expiry: \(\mathrm{HACK}+\mathrm{NOHACK} = \$1\) enforces price conservation
  3. Risk neutrality: the HACK price equals the risk-neutral exploit probability; misreporting beliefs is unprofitable

Proposition 2 · A minimum yield share sustains stable participation

A minimum yield share \(\gamma_{\min}\) sustains LP participation: \(r_{\mathrm{LP}} \ge r_{\mathrm{market}} + \rho_{\mathrm{LP}}\). The capital–utilization–return feedback is self-stabilizing.
\(\gamma_{\min} = \dfrac{C_{\mathrm{LP}}\,(r_{\mathrm{market}}+\rho_{\mathrm{LP}}) + p_{\mathrm{hack}}\,\mathbb{E}\bigl[(\min(\mathrm{coverage},\mathrm{Loss})-C_{\mathrm{C}})^{+}\bigr]}{(1-\varphi)\,r_{\mathrm{pool}}\,C_{\mathrm{total}}}\) (Eq. 14)
  1. LP return: yield share net of expected covered losses beyond first-loss collateral, per unit of capital
  2. Imposing the outside option \(r_{\mathrm{market}}+\rho_{\mathrm{LP}}\) and solving for \(\gamma\) yields the participation bound
  3. Adjustment \(\partial C_{\mathrm{LP}}/\partial t = \kappa_{\mathrm{LP}} C_{\mathrm{LP}}\,[\,r_{\mathrm{LP}}(U) - (r_{\mathrm{market}}+\rho_{\mathrm{LP}})\,]\) (Eq. 15): returns above the bound attract capital, utilization falls, the yield share compresses

Proposition 3 · The dynamic cap enforces the solvency bound

The pool remains solvent with probability \(\ge 1-\varepsilon\) whenever \(U \le 1/(\psi_{1-\varepsilon}\, c_{\max})\); the dynamic cap \(U_{\max}(t)\) can enforce this bound.
\(\mathrm{payout}_{\max} \le \psi_{1-\varepsilon}\, c_{\max}\, U\, C_{\mathrm{LP}} \le C_{\mathrm{LP}} \;\Longleftrightarrow\; U \le \dfrac{1}{\psi_{1-\varepsilon}\, c_{\max}}\) (Eq. 17)
  1. Single hack per epoch: the obligation is bounded by the largest coverage, at most \(c_{\max}\cdot U\cdot C_{\mathrm{LP}}\)
  2. Bounding the payout by pool capital at the \((1-\varepsilon)\)-quantile \(\psi_{1-\varepsilon}\) of loss-to-coverage yields the utilization bound
  3. With \(k\) hacks per epoch, \(c_{\max}\) becomes the sum of the \(k\) largest coverage shares (online appendix)

Discussion

Open design questions and further research

  • Operator decentralization — verification runs off-chain via a trusted operator; licensing and multi-signature governance mitigate, DAO-based verification is a future iteration
  • Relaxing the idealizations — beyond the single-hack epoch (\(k\)-hack generalization in the appendix) and risk-neutral speculators; the stress test is a first probe
  • Collusion and manipulation control — arbitrage makes price manipulation costly, independent speculator pricing deters protocol–LP collusion; Sybil circumvention and insider access need operational enforcement
  • Operating the forward markets — launch and rollover of quarterly expiries: batch auction or capped bonding curve at the bootstrap, seeded market-making, seed withdrawal at depth thresholds
  • Deployment — protocol-level speculator liquidity, a staged rollout from high-TVL protocols, regulatory clarity on event markets
  • Ongoing extensions — attacker cost economics with empirically retrieved loss severities, and an implementation of the mechanism as a dynamic attacker–mechanism game

Conclusion

The coalition that rescued Kelp DAO is this mechanism's natural LP base. The mechanism organizes the same capital ex ante and at market prices.

Equilibrium exists (Thm 1, Props 1–3) · in a stylized simulation the mechanism self-stabilizes and no insolvency occurs · runs on established prediction-market technology
